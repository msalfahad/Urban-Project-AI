What the engine has learned, and how far it is from the product

Two villas measured from their own drawings, one of them blind and then checked against a historical workbook. This is everything the system knows, everything it got wrong on the way, and the honest distance left to a web app Urban can put in front of a client.

Revision 2 carries an external audit's corrections. The rules it now carries

The distinction that matters: a number you give for one villa stays with that villa, and a rule you approve travels to every future project. They are stored separately so the next project inherits the rule and not the number.

The priority ladder

Consulted strictly in order, and a lower rank is never used while a higher one can answer:

  • 1 — A dimension written on the drawing.
  • 2 — A dimension measured from the drawing geometry.
  • 3 — An explicit owner value for this project.
  • 4 — An approved Urban standard.
  • 5 — Ask the owner. Nothing is invented at this rank.

What "no engine errors" does and does not mean. It is the verdict of the historical validation: no difference against the historical workbook proved a frozen quantity wrong. It never meant the engine makes no mistakes. The external audit of the detailed export went on to find four classification defects — pending floor area published as porcelain, components published as rooms, a ceiling finish marked final, and a guide category used outside its band — all of which are corrected in revision 2 and are now covered by tests.

Two other things went right that are harder to put in a table. The engine found and fixed its own 155 m² defect before any comparison with history. And when a 348% aluminium gap appeared — exactly where an eager validator declares an engine error — it went back to the drawing, counted 1,859 and 1,334 drawn paths on the basement and roof plans, found zero glazed openings there, and classified the difference as scope rather than error.

What it got wrong

Every one of these was found and fixed inside the work. I am listing them because the pattern they make is more useful than any of them alone: the engine's failures are nearly always a rule applied one level too confidently, not arithmetic.

Rooms leaked through doorways DEF-01 fixed

Symptom
One "room" of 425 m² and another of 560 m² — the whole floor flooding through open doorways.
Cause
A door is a hole in a wall, and the flood fill treated the hole as an absence of wall.
Fix
Virtual closures: a gap up to 3.00 m in a wall line is spanned for the purpose of bounding a room, and is never wall material.
Lesson
Topology and material are two different questions about the same line.

Tile preparation under-measured by 155 m² DEF-02 fixed

Symptom
Wall porcelain and the preparation behind it disagreed by 155.340 m², and the first instinct was to call it a trade-scope difference.
Cause
The face count required a wall line to span a whole grid cell, so walls drawn in fragments were missed.
Fix
Both trades take the same host: the room's perimeter less its own openings. Difference now 0.000.
Lesson
When two measurements of the same physical face disagree, one of them is wrong. Do not write a rule to excuse the gap.

159 "openings" that were mostly junctions DEF-03 fixed

Cause
Every gap in a wall line was read as an opening. Most gaps are where one wall meets another.
Fix
An opening is a gap with a door or window symbol in it. Rebuilt from jamb marks and block symbols: 37.

"The window widths are unavailable" DEF-04 fixed

Symptom
I concluded and reported that no window width could be measured, and proposed to use the guide's widths.
Cause
Dynamic blocks do not expand in the decode, and the path coordinates I first used were in a different space from the text.
Fix
The plot draws each glazed opening as a white rectangle punched through the wall. All seven widths were measurable after all.
Lesson
"Not available" was a statement about my method, not about the drawing. It cost a near-miss where a standard would have replaced a measurement.

The PDF-to-DWG transform returned nonsense DEF-05 fixed

Cause
A similarity transform with positive scale cannot express a reflection, and the PDF's y axis runs down while the drawing's runs up.
Fix
Negate page y before fitting. Residual dropped to 0.021 m on withheld witnesses.

Multi-word room labels were invisible DEF-06 fixed

Cause
Words were grouped on a shared y. The plans are rotated 90°, so "BED ROOM" shares an x instead.
Fix
Group on either axis.

CLOSE_AGREEMENT was used too generously DEF-07 corrected

Symptom
I reported 3.8%, 5.9% and 9.1% differences as close agreement. You set the band at 2%.
Cause
A judgement where a threshold belonged. Generosity in a validator is a defect, not a courtesy.
Fix
2% is now arithmetic. Three of six comparisons left the class and each had to name its cause. Two of seven differences are inside the band — a smaller and truer number than the one I first gave you.

A floor was inferred where none was stated DEF-08 corrected

Symptom
I read rows 5–21 of the historical schedule as basement openings.
Cause
The sheet has one header at the top and nothing marking where the ground floor begins. I filled the gap with a reading.
Fix
Four rows of thirty-one have proof. The other twenty-seven are FLOOR = UNKNOWN. The rule US-22 exists so Urban's own schedules never repeat it.

A commercial basis was called a quantity DEF-09 corrected

Symptom
950.22 m² was carried as a "historical waterproofing quantity".
Cause
It is SUM(F5:F24) feeding =1.5*F28, and one of its twenty components is 600.00 — the plot area. It is a priced basis, not a membrane area.
Fix
Recorded as HISTORICAL_COMMERCIAL_BASIS, and it may not be compared against a measured area at all.

Pending floor area was published as porcelain DEF-11 fixed in rev 2

Symptom
The JSON export carried 62 PORCELAIN_FLOOR records totalling 931.016 m², while the workbook and BOQ correctly used 734.638 m². The extra 196.378 m² is 9 stair and landing components and 24 unnamed spaces.
Cause
One role list did the work of two questions: what is enclosed, and what takes porcelain. A stair takes treads; an unnamed space takes whatever the owner decides.
Fix
Those 33 records are now FLOOR_FINISH_UNCLASSIFIED / AR-FL-PENDING / FINISH_CLASSIFICATION_PENDING, BOQ_INCLUDED: false. The area is kept and measured; it is simply not porcelain until somebody says so.

135 components were called 135 rooms DEF-12 fixed in rev 2

Symptom
Every artifact, and this report, said "135 rooms measured". 71 of those are wall material and slivers — 54.7 m² of them.
Fix
A published census: 135 connected components = 64 non-sliver + 71 wall/sliver, with role counts (16 internal, 12 wet, 1 kitchen, 9 stair, 24 unnamed, 2 external). A connected component becomes a room when the drawing names it or its geometry proves what it is, and not before.

A guide category used outside its own band DEF-13 fixed in rev 2

Symptom
Window AR-W-05 sits in a 91.06 m² hall and was given the LARGE_HALL category, whose band is 35–60 m². Its 2.20 m height was published as if settled.
Fix
Every window now carries an explicit authority. A category matched from a drawing label is supported by the label (which is why GR-108's M.BED ROOM overrides the area band). A category chosen from area is supported only inside its band; outside it, the height is ASK_THE_OWNER and the window moves to AR-AL-PENDING. The two aluminium lines still add back to the frozen 14.3301 m².

Two tests asserted yesterday's world DEF-10 fixed

Symptom
A test asserted that no villa files were present; another asserted that no workbook had been opened. Both were true when written and false afterwards.
Fix
Rewritten to assert the rule — that the workflow stops without a villa set, that no historical figure reached the frozen quantities — rather than the state of one afternoon.

What I need from you

Eight questions, none of them previously answered. Each one unblocks a quantity or a rule; none of them is a matter of taste.

1. Skirting — is it in scope, and at what height?

828.49 lm derived from the frozen geometry, and since revision 2 split into three buckets rather than offered as one number: 381.535 lm in dry named internal spaces (candidates), 97.355 lm on stairs and landings and 349.603 lm in unnamed spaces (both blocked until their floor finish is settled). Every line is DERIVED_NOT_IN_FROZEN_TAKEOFF, provisional, and priced nowhere.

If skirting is standard Urban scope, this becomes a rule and every future villa gets it automatically.

2. What finish do the stairs and the unnamed spaces take?

67.6 m² of stair and landing, and 128.8 m² the drawing does not name. Both are measured exactly and both sit outside the floor-finish line.

Nearly 200 m² of floor finish hangs on this — more than a quarter of the internal area.

3. The external and parking areas — 155.2 m². What material?

The area is final. AR-08 told me to measure them and not invent a finish, so the line reads FINISH_CLASSIFICATION_PENDING.

Interlock, tiles and plain screed are different trades at different rates.

4. What goes on the roof above the waterproofing?

474.7 m² of roof, with the membrane measured and the finish unstated.

5. Is wet-room floor tanking standard Urban scope even when it is not drawn?

No detail on any of the three plans. If you say it is standard, it becomes a rule like US-18 and applies to 91.7 m² of wet and service floor here.

This is exactly the shape of question that should end in a travelling rule rather than a project answer.

6. Who supplies the site-scope opening register?

The drawings support 7 windows. The site record listed 34 objects. I can publish both registers, but only if the second one comes from a source Urban controls — a measured site survey, not a historical price list.

7. Are there suspended or decorative ceilings?

931.0 m² is measured as ceiling plan area. The area is final; the ceiling type is not, so since revision 2 the finish carries FINISH_CLASSIFICATION_PENDING and sits in the provisional section of the BOQ rather than being priced as a plain ceiling.

8. Waste percentages and rates — do you have Urban standards?

The workbook has the columns, wired with the formulas, and deliberately empty. Tell me the standard waste per trade and I will make them rules with the same travel discipline as everything else.

Also: if you can send the window size guide PDF itself, I will check it against the table you transcribed rather than assume they match.

The web app

Not started, on your instruction. Here is what I would build and, more usefully, what is already done for it.

What already exists

  • The data model. The 265-record export is the database schema, DS-01-shaped: every record carries project, drawing revision, floor, room, trade, item, components, calculation, measured quantity, unit, waste, procurement quantity, rate, amount, source, rule id, status and approval status.
  • The rule store. Urban standards, project values and schema rules are already separate objects with a travel flag.
  • The audit trail. Frozen artifacts with digests, plus a validation amendment that changes the record without touching the measurement. That is version history with a reason attached.
  • The review material. Every quantity decomposes to rectangles a reviewer can check by eye.

What I would build, in order

  • Upload and identify. Drop a DWG and a PDF; the app runs decode, units and region isolation and shows what it found. The one human step: confirm the plan windows when the containment score is below 1.000.
  • Review the rooms. The plan with each room's rectangles drawn over it, its area, and one click to name an unnamed space or correct a role. Every override recorded as an owner input, never silently merged into the measurement.
  • Answer the blockers. The open questions, as a queue: wall height, door height, finish materials. The engine already knows which quantities each answer unblocks.
  • The BOQ. The thirteen sheets as screens, with the same live formulas and the same empty price columns until a rate library exists.
  • Approve. Records leave the engine as DRAFT and only a person moves them to approved. That is a product rule, not a technical one.

What I would not build yet

Anything that prices automatically. A rate applied to a quantity nobody has reviewed is how the historical workbook ended up with =1100+325+430+150*2.5, and that cell is the reason DS-01 exists.
